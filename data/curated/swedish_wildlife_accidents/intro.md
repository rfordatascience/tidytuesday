This week we're exploring wildlife-vehicle collisions in Sweden. Drivers in Sweden are required by law to report collisions with large wild animals to the police, and the reports are followed up at the accident site by trained hunters from the National Wildlife Accident Council (Nationella viltolycksrådet). This dataset contains the roughly 156,000 accidents reported between July 2024 and June 2026, one row per animal involved, with the species, time, location, and what happened to the animal.

The council's statistics portal highlights some patterns to look for:

- When during the year do most accidents happen? According to the council, October and November are when most wildlife accidents happen in Sweden. Can you confirm this in the data? Is the seasonal peak the same for all species?
- When during the day do most accidents happen? The council says most accidents generally happen between 06:00 and 07:00. Does the data agree? How does the time-of-day pattern shift with the seasons and daylight hours?
- Where do accidents with different species cluster geographically? What might explain the differences?
