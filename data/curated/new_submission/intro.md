This week we're exploring documented Catholic miracles spanning nearly two millennia. The dataset contains 1,990 events across 6 categories — Marian apparitions, Eucharistic miracles, miraculous images, incorrupt bodies, stigmata, and Lourdes healing cures — scraped from three primary source websites: [MiracleHunter.com](https://www.miraclehunter.com), the [Carlo Acutis Eucharistic Miracles Exhibition](https://www.miracolieucaristici.org), and the [Official Lourdes Sanctuary](https://www.lourdes-france.com).

What makes this dataset interesting is the Church's own filtering process. Of 808 Marian apparition claims since 1900, fewer than 25 received positive rulings — a rejection rate over 96%. At Lourdes, only 72 of 7,000+ reported cures have been declared "medically inexplicable." The dataset captures both approved events and rejected/uninvestigated claims, giving a complete picture of how selective the Church's discernment process actually is.

> "Although uncommon, the miraculous cures are evidence of somatic and mental processes we do not know." — Bernard François, Esther M. Sternberg, and Elizabeth Fee, *Journal of the History of Medicine and Allied Sciences* (2014), reviewing the Lourdes medical evidence.
