The Visual Studio Code Marketplace publishes an install count on every extension page.
That is unusual: most catalogues of things make you guess at demand from reviews or stars,
and this one states it. This week's data is a crawl of 64,464 extensions from
50,446 publishers, taken in August 2026, with those install counts attached.

The distribution is about as unequal as distributions get. The top 1% of extensions hold
87.4% of all installs in the sample and the top 10% hold
98.0%; the median extension has 804 installs and the
largest has 231,321,041. At least 54.4% have fewer than a
thousand. Two other threads are worth pulling: 65.2% of extensions have not
been updated in twelve months, and the newest categories are the most crowded and the least
installed — the AI category holds 2,040 extensions with a
median of 39 installs, against
1,865 for Programming Languages.

**One caveat matters for every claim you make from this.** The crawl walked the Marketplace's
own category taxonomy sorted by install count and paged until a cap, so categories larger
than that cap are truncated **from the top**: the long tail is under-represented. Every
"under N installs" percentage here is therefore a **floor**, not an estimate, and the
concentration figures are conservative — a fuller tail would make the head's share larger,
not smaller. It is a sample, not a census, and the sampling is documented in the source.
